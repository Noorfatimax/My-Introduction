The problem

The problem

A pharmacy marketplace only works if every person sees exactly what they should, and can trust everything they can't see.

Safer Health connects pharmacies, doctors and patients, with a Super Admin overseeing all of it. Money moves through the doctor's Stripe account, pharmacies are paid after a 14-day hold, and the platform sits in the middle. Providers and pharmacies never see each other.

I worked on the three seams where that trust is most fragile: who gets to prescribe (provider management), what a prescription says (directions, or SIG), and what can be sold and by whom (products). Every one of them touches all four roles, and each role needs a different view.

Principles & constraints

Three rules that shaped every screen

01

The platform is the middleman

Roles can't open each other's detail pages. Hidden things are simply absent, not greyed out.

02

Reversible and attributed

Nothing active is hard-deleted. Deactivate or archive instead, and every admin action carries the admin's name.

03

Three gates to order

A product is orderable only if admin visibility, pharmacy status and the patient's state licence all pass.

Provider management · Admin

Letting an admin trust a doctor, and undo it safely

Decision 01 · Review lifecycle

Four statuses, one reversible path

Providers move through Pending Review, Active, Declined and Inactive. Declining needs a reason so the doctor can fix and resubmit. Deactivating stops new orders and patient assignments at once, while accepted orders are still fulfilled.

I chose deactivate over delete, with a confirmation step, because orders in flight still have to be fulfilled and every action needs an audit trail.

Decision 02 · Financials

Refunds that stay inside the system

A patient refund goes back into the provider's Credits Reserved, and the next order deducts credits first. Cash refunds happen off-platform, so the admin records them as a manual ledger entry. Add Entry, Manual Adjustment and both refund types live in one tab, next to the pricing tier (10, 20 or 30%, or custom).

I chose credits over cash refunds because the provider is merchant of record and money must reconcile on the ledger.

Directions (SIG) · Every role

Directions that are fast to pick and hard to get wrong

Decision 03 · The builder

A sentence you build, or free text, never both at once

Directions are built as an inline sentence of dropdown and number chips, with correct pluralisation and one optional instruction. A template can also be custom directions only. Editing the full text switches to custom mode and hides the builder, and "Reset to builder" brings it back.

I chose one active mode over two parallel inputs because it removes the case where the sentence and the builder disagree.

Decision 04 · Inside a prescription

One field that searches, filters and falls back

When a doctor configures a medication, one field lists directions for that medication from pharmacy templates and their own library. Typing filters them, "+ SIG Builder" opens the builder, and no match becomes free-text custom directions. Patients only read the result.

I moved from tabs to a single combined field after testing options with the client, who preferred it. Custom directions don't auto-save to the library; only the builder does.

Product management · Every role

Deciding what can be sold, and keeping it safe to change

Decision 05 · Approval lifecycle

Pending first, live only when approved

Pharmacies submit products with variations, pricing and directions. Nothing is selectable by doctors until the admin approves. A decline requires a reason, and the pharmacy can edit and resubmit. A product is orderable only if admin visibility, pharmacy status and the patient's state licence all pass.

I chose default-allow visibility with admin overrides over per-product grants because it keeps the catalog usable while still letting admin restrict.

Decision 06 · One shared taxonomy

Admin owns the lists, pharmacies pick from them

Category, route, dosage form and package type are standardised by the admin. Dosage forms are a tree (route → form → package) with archive instead of delete, inline rename and product-count tooltips. Archiving cascades, and restoring a parent restores its children.

I chose archive over delete because products and orders still reference these values.

Decision 07 · Changing a live product

Stay live on the old values until the new ones are approved

When a pharmacy edits a live product, it keeps selling on the old values while the change waits in an admin review queue. The admin sees previous and updated values and accepts, or declines with a reason. The existing price-change request folds into the same flow.

I chose approval for every field because a small change to a medication can be a safety change. Still open with the client: direct edit vs request box.
